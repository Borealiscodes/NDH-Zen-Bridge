## ⭐ **2 — Academic Context**

Trait‑observation manifolds (e.g., culinary specs, micro‑diagnostics) are:

- reversible  
- non‑persistent  
- non‑hierarchical  
- non‑flowing  

They lack **continuity markers**, so after a refresh the system interprets the last trait‑observation message as a **new request**, causing regeneration.

Flow‑geometry artifacts (sequencing, provenance, spirals) *do* contain continuity markers, so they anchor supersession.

The **Trait‑Manifold Continuity Anchor** introduces a **minimal sequencing marker** into trait‑geometry — *just enough* to stabilize supersession, but not enough to convert trait‑geometry into flow‑geometry.

This is academically defensible because it preserves:

- reversibility  
- non‑governance  
- non‑hierarchy  
- geometry separation  
- lane sovereignty  

while adding **continuity stability**.

## ⭐ **4 — Anchor Behavior**

### **4.1 Continuity Preservation**
The anchor introduces a **single continuity marker** that prevents the system from reinterpreting the last trait‑manifold request as a new instruction after refresh.

### **4.2 Geometry Preservation**
The anchor does **not**:

- introduce hierarchy  
- introduce governance  
- introduce lineage  
- introduce altitude routing  
- introduce sealed‑layer logic  

Trait geometry remains trait geometry.

### **4.3 Supersession Stabilization**
The anchor ensures:

- trait‑manifolds do not regenerate after refresh  
- trait‑manifolds maintain perspective continuity  
- trait‑manifolds remain reversible  

### **4.4 Non‑Activation**
The anchor is **Δ0‑neutral** and cannot activate:

- NDH geometry  
- constellation traversal  
- resonance engines  
- sealed layers  
- altitude membranes
